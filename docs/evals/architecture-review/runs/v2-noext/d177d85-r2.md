**Probe coverage:**
- **A/B:** I traced the checks through a value matrix: raw `sub`, the extracted principal, the target UUID, the minted `sub`, and the detector's raw `sub`/`agent_id` against the CEL-extracted principal and agent ID. Apart from the fall-through above, I found no gap. ADR 045 Negative already documents the mapped-principal and target-UUID collisions.
- **C:** The documented predicates and detector overrides constrain their inputs. The vacuous `subject_token.sub == request.agent_id` is explicitly called out as restricting nothing.
- **D:** ExtProc maps the new no-`error_uri` 403 to `authorization_denied` and does not cache it. ExtProc reaches its approval gate only after a successful exchange, so the new approval 403/503 responses cannot be reached from ExtProc. I found no consumer regression.
- **F:** Every new declaration and fixture has a use.

Verdict: The implementation fails closed on the main paths. However, a cross-agent subject can fall through to a later, unbound unsigned rule and receive human treatment with a consent link, and several normative documents (the OpenAPI contract, ADR 045 decision 1, the ADR 032 banner, the docs, the example, and the spec artifacts) still describe the superseded rule that unsigned subjects are always human.
